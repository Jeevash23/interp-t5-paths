## New since the 20 Oct survey

Every item dated after 13 October 2026 that touches FlyVis, T5 or the hypotheses, whether or not it is a scoop:

- **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23553452** (software, 22 Oct 2026; the author's own record). "Silencing in the FlyVis models matches fly experiments for Mi1 and Mi9 but not for the OFF-pathway inputs: code and results". P7's code, saved outputs and frozen protocols. It is the author's own precursor to BM2, not a scoop.
- **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23553590** (software, 22 Oct 2026; the author's own record). "First-order estimates find the influential cell types in the FlyVis models but misjudge their silencing effects: code and results". It compares gradient estimates of silencing with exact silencing of all 65 types and traces the errors to the nonlinearity of the direction-selectivity readout. This is the author's own work, but it bears on BM2's readout comparison, which BM2 should cite and keep distinct from.
- **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23485345** (software, 20 Oct 2026; record created 19 Oct). Avendaño Castro, Llacsahuache Ortiz, Calderón Chávez, Alcalde Tirado and Acosta Medina, a collision detector modelled on the fly's escape circuit, for public transport in Lima (ICSIC 2026). LPLC2 weights come from FlyWire, flyvis is one detector variant, and weights are ablated. It does not touch T5's inputs.
- **Zhou and Hasler, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.06.10.731214 v12** (16 Oct 2026). Already known, and still the latest version: representational geometry of FlyVis against weight-shuffled and rewired nulls, with no silencing.
- **TuragaLab/flyvis pull requests #28, #29 and #30** (merged 14 Oct 2026). Fixes to the T4/T5 tuning metrics, ensemble caching and training resume (row above). They are not a study.
- **GitHub, new FlyVis projects:** lemonmeat/flyvis (19 Oct), NotArsenic/FlyByWire (20 Oct), VibhuRaj01/FlyVis-vs-CNN (22 Oct) and Bartusivaci/retinal-waves-flyvis (16 Oct). Each uses FlyVis as a front end or toy, and none perturbs T5's inputs.
- **Tangential:** Yad and Nabila, arXiv 2610.29205 (21 Oct 2026), "Reconstruction differences rival individual differences in the fly compass connectome and break trained circuit models". Trained head-direction models break when all weights change by 1%, while real compasses do not. The theme is model robustness against the animal's, but it covers neither the visual system nor FlyVis.

Nothing new was found from Thomas Ryan, Stefanidi et al., the Turaga or Macke groups, Karaneen et al., FlyHack or flyvisu after 13 October.

**Verdict: not found.** No work found cuts Tm→T5 or Tm→CT1 synapses, replays T5's input with only one Tm type's direct input removed, silences the Tm1+Tm2+Tm4+Tm9 quadruple, or compares T5 readouts to explain why T5 survives Tm silencing in FlyVis. The closest items are as the survey recorded. Ryan's Paper 13 (10.5281/zenodo.23352620) tests random higher-order sets with the central T5 peak as readout, but not the Tm quadruple and no path cuts. Ryan's Paper 11 (10.5281/zenodo.23050532) states the redundancy and inhibition hypotheses without testing them. FlyHack (arXiv 2610.13018) runs single-connection interventions in FlyVis, but only to score attribution methods, not on T5's inputs. The author's own first-order record (10.5281/zenodo.23553590) touches the readout question. The cell-level silencing that motivates BM2 is done (Ryan Papers 11–12, P7); the path-level explanation is not. Confidence is medium-high for Zenodo, arXiv and bioRxiv up to 22 October, but medium overall, for four reasons: the bioRxiv search page was blocked (a category scan through the API replaced it), GitHub code search was unavailable, manuscripts were not read, and Ryan's weekly series is overdue.
